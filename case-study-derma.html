DermaScreen AI

Smartphone-Based Skin Cancer Detection

Role

AI/ML Engineer Intern

Timeline

July 2026 – Oct 2026

Stack

PyTorchComputer VisionPython

The Problem

In many developing nations and resource-constrained environments, access to certified dermatologists is severely limited. Skin cancer, when detected late, has a dramatically lower survival rate. Current AI diagnostic tools often rely on high-resolution dermatoscopic images taken with specialized clinical equipment, rendering them useless for the average patient with a standard smartphone.

The challenge was to build a robust diagnostic pipeline that could achieve clinical-grade accuracy using standard, non-dermatoscopic images taken in varying lighting conditions by regular users.

The Beneficiaries

Primary: Patients in rural or underserved areas who cannot easily travel to a specialist. The application serves as an early-warning triage system, alerting them when a lesion is highly suspicious and requires immediate professional medical intervention.

Secondary: General practitioners at primary care clinics who can use the tool as a second opinion when deciding whether to refer a patient to an oncology or dermatology specialist.

Technical Implementation

During my deep-tech internship, I architected the core machine learning pipeline. I aggregated and pre-processed massive datasets including PAD-UFES-20, HAM10000, and ISIC 2024. A major hurdle was the inherent class imbalance in medical datasets (benign lesions vastly outnumber malignant ones).

I utilized advanced data augmentation techniques and focal loss functions within a PyTorch environment to train Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). To address the poor lighting and blur of smartphone cameras, I implemented an automated image-quality assessment and contrast enhancement pre-processing step before the image reaches the classifier.

Outcome & Impact

The final model achieved a significant increase in sensitivity and specificity for melanoma detection on non-dermatoscopic images compared to baseline models. By successfully deploying this as a lightweight, on-device model, we completely eliminated the need for cloud-processing, ensuring patient privacy and enabling use in offline, remote areas.
