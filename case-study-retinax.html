The Problem

Diabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of blindness globally, yet its early detection relies heavily on manual grading of retinal fundus images by highly trained ophthalmologists. In areas with high patient volumes and a shortage of specialists, the delay in screening often results in irreversible vision loss before treatment can begin. A critical issue with existing AI solutions in this domain is their "black-box" nature — doctors are hesitant to trust a diagnosis when they cannot see the AI's reasoning.

The Beneficiaries

Primary: Ophthalmologists and clinical staff who can use this tool to rapidly triage patient scans, prioritizing cases that show severe progression.

Secondary: Diabetic patients who receive faster, standardized diagnostic results, significantly increasing their chances of early, preventative treatment.

Technical Implementation

We built an end-to-end automated screening pipeline. The first and most crucial step is image pre-processing. Since clinical fundus cameras often produce unevenly lit images, we implemented CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) to normalize contrast across the retinal surface without amplifying background noise.

Once enhanced, the images are fed into a customized neural network architecture designed to segment specific lesions (such as microaneurysms, hemorrhages, and hard exudates). Finally, to solve the "black-box" problem, we integrated Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping). This generates a heatmap overlay directly on the original fundus image, visibly highlighting the exact lesions the model focused on to output its ICDR severity grade.

Outcome & Impact

The pipeline successfully automated the International Clinical Diabetic Retinopathy (ICDR) severity grading with high concordance to expert manual grading. The inclusion of Grad-CAM heatmaps proved to be a game-changer for clinical trust, translating abstract AI predictions into transparent, clinically actionable visual insights.
